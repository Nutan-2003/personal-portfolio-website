Understanding Perceptual Hashing

Posted on June 15, 2024 | 5 min read

Introduction

Perceptual hashing is a technique for generating a fingerprint of a multimedia file (such as an image) that is robust to small changes, such as resizing or compression. This allows for efficient image comparison and duplicate detection. In this post, I’ll explore how perceptual hashing works and its applications, drawing from my academic project experience.

Project Overview

In my project, I analyzed the performance of perceptual hashing algorithms and deep learning techniques for identifying near-duplicate images. I evaluated robustness against transformations such as resizing, cropping, and color changes.

Key Findings

  • Deep learning models achieve higher accuracy, especially with complex image variations.
  • Perceptual hashing provides faster computation and is efficient for large-scale image datasets.
  • There are trade-offs between efficiency and precision, which must be considered depending on the application.

Applications

This research is useful for plagiarism detection, content moderation, and organizing large image datasets. It also has applications in AI and computer vision, areas I am passionate about as a CSE student.

Conclusion

Working on this project deepened my understanding of both traditional algorithms and modern AI techniques. I look forward to applying these insights in future research and development projects.
