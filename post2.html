Building My First Portfolio

Posted on May 28, 2024 | 3 min read

Introduction

As a Computer Science and Engineering student at SRM Institute of Science and Technology, I have always been passionate about building real-world solutions and showcasing my journey. Creating my first portfolio website was both an exciting and educational experience, allowing me to combine my technical skills in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with my interest in clean, user-friendly design.

Planning and Purpose

The main goal for my portfolio was to present my academic background, technical skills, and project experience in a professional and accessible way. I wanted the site to serve as a dynamic extension of my resume-demonstrating not just what I know, but what I can build. I also aimed to make it easy for recruiters and collaborators to get in touch with me.

Design and Technology Choices

Given my proficiency in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, I chose to build the site from scratch to ensure full control over the layout and interactivity. I focused on a clean, modern look with intuitive navigation, making sure the site is responsive and accessible on all devices. I included sections for my education, major projects, technical skills, achievements, and contact information.

Showcasing My Projects

One of the highlights of my portfolio is the Projects section, where I detail key academic and independent works:

  • Comparative Analysis of Perceptual Hashing and Deep Learning for Image Detection: I evaluated different algorithms for near-duplicate image detection, highlighting the trade-offs between speed and accuracy.
  • MarineDetect: I developed a CNN-based model for ship identification in diverse ocean environments, optimizing for real-time maritime surveillance.
  • Faucet Plants Process Benchmarking: I analyzed manufacturing workflows and suggested automation strategies for productivity improvement.

Overcoming Challenges

Balancing technical depth with clear communication was a key challenge. I wanted each project description to be understandable to both technical and non-technical audiences. I also ensured the site was optimized for speed and mobile usability, applying best practices I learned from coursework and workshops.

Lessons Learned

  • Quality over Quantity: I selected projects that best demonstrate my growth and abilities, rather than listing every assignment.
  • Continuous Learning: Building the portfolio motivated me to learn new web technologies and design trends, keeping my skills current.
  • Personal Branding: The process helped me clarify my strengths in problem-solving, teamwork, and quick learning-qualities reinforced by my academic achievements and extracurricular interests like badminton and basketball.

Conclusion

Building my first portfolio website was more than a technical exercise-it was a reflection of my journey as a student, developer, and problem-solver. I look forward to updating it as I gain new experiences and skills, and I hope it serves as a gateway to future opportunities in software engineering and AI.
